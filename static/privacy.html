Privacy

Door Kick Watch is a community project for tracking door-kicking incidents. It isn’t run by the police, but moderators share reports with local police to help them investigate. This page explains what we collect when you send a report and who can see it.

What everyone can see

  • The block, not the house: for example “1200 block of Main St”. The map point is moved to the centre of a square about 150 m (500 ft) across.
  • The date and time it happened.
  • How many people were involved, whether there was damage, and whether footage exists.
  • Whether a moderator has verified the report.

What only moderators and police can see

  • The exact address you typed and the exact pin location.
  • Anything you put in the private fields: the police report number, your description, and your email or phone number.

Moderators may send these details to local police. Police may contact you if you left contact details, for example to ask for camera footage.

What we don’t collect

  • No accounts, names, photos or videos.
  • No tracking or advertising cookies.
  • Your location when you press Near me. That check happens entirely in your browser.

To limit spam, we store a one-way scrambled version of your IP address with each report. It’s used only to stop one device sending too many reports.

Other services this site uses

  • Address search: addresses you search for are looked up with OpenStreetMap’s Nominatim service. Our server sends the search for you, so OpenStreetMap doesn’t see your IP address.
  • Map images: your browser loads map images directly from OpenStreetMap, which can see your IP address the same way any website you visit does. See the OpenStreetMap privacy policy.

Removing or correcting a report

Reports are kept so neighbours and police can see patterns over time. To correct or remove a report you sent, contact the site operator with the address and the approximate date and time.

Please keep in mind

  • Don’t name or describe the people involved. Many are minors. Give footage to police directly.
  • This site isn’t monitored in real time. If it’s happening now, call your local emergency number (e.g. 911).
